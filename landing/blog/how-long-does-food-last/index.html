How Long Does Food Last? A Practical Storage Guide

Dates on packs are a starting point. Storage, temperature and what you do with food matter more than the printed day.

By leftovr 8 min read Updated Nov 10, 2026
Cooked leftovers last three to four days in the fridge. Raw meat, fish and fresh produce vary by item, and dates on packs are guides, not guarantees. Keep the fridge at 40°F (4°C) or below and freeze food before it turns.

Key takeaways

  • Cooked leftovers keep three to four days in the fridge, per USDA guidance.
  • Keep the fridge at 40°F (4°C) or colder and the freezer at 0°F (-18°C).
  • Refrigerate cooked food within two hours.
  • "Use by" and "best before" mean different things. Treat use-by on raw meat and fish seriously.
  • Freezing stops the clock. Do it before food goes off, not after.

How long do leftovers last in the fridge?

Three to four days. That is the guidance from the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service. It also says to get cooked food into the fridge within two hours (one hour if the air is above 90°F) and to reheat leftovers to 165°F.

Count from the day you cooked, not the day you opened the container. If you will not eat it inside four days, freeze it on day one or two, in portions you can thaw separately.

What temperature should the fridge and freezer be?

40°F (4°C) or below for the fridge, 0°F (-18°C) or below for the freezer. Both numbers come from the same USDA guidance. A cheap appliance thermometer is the only way to know, because the dial settings are rarely accurate.

Frozen food held at 0°F stays safe indefinitely. Freezer time limits, such as three to four months for leftovers, are about quality: taste and texture fade, but the food does not become unsafe.

What is the difference between use by and best before?

Use by is about safety. Best before is about quality. A best-before date tells you when the maker thinks the food is at its best. Eating it a little after is usually fine if it looks, smells and tastes normal and has been stored properly. A use-by date on raw meat, fish or ready-to-eat chilled foods is a different matter: respect it, and do not rely on a smell test.

Date labelling rules vary by country, so the exact wording on your pack may differ from these terms.

Roughly how long do common foods last?

It depends on the item, how it was stored and whether it was opened. Treat the table as a rough guide for planning what to eat first, not a safety rule. The pack date, your fridge temperature and your own senses override it.

Which foods should you eat first?

Anything fresh, cut, cooked or opened. Rank your kitchen in three tiers:

  • Today or tomorrow: raw fish, minced meat, cut fruit, cooked leftovers on day three.
  • This week: leafy greens, herbs, berries, opened dairy, fresh bread.
  • Later: whole root vegetables, eggs, hard cheese, dry goods, tins.

The tiers matter more than the exact number of days. Eat tier one before you open tier three.

How can you make food last longer?

Cool it, dry it, wrap it, or freeze it.

  • Cool cooked food quickly in shallow containers.
  • Store cut produce dry and airtight.
  • Keep raw meat on the bottom shelf in a sealed container so juices cannot drip.
  • Freeze bread, meat and cooked portions early, and label them with a date.
  • Do not wash berries or greens until you use them.

When in doubt

If you are unsure about cooked or high-risk food, throw it out. A few pounds of food is cheaper than a day of illness. For low-risk items like bread or hard cheese, use your eyes and nose.

How do you tell if food has gone off?

Look, smell, and check the date, in that order. Mould, slime, sour smells and fizzing in sealed products are clear signs to throw food away. But harmful bacteria do not always change how food looks or smells, which is why time and temperature rules matter more for cooked and high-risk food than your nose.

Never taste food to check it. If you cannot decide, the cost of replacing it is small compared with the risk.

What about eggs, bread and dry goods?

They are the slow movers, so they rarely cause trouble. Eggs, whole root vegetables and dry goods last far longer than fresh produce and cooked food, which is why they belong in the "later" tier. The mistake is the opposite one: ignoring them for so long that they hide fresher food that needed eating.

Frequently asked questions

How long can cooked food stay in the fridge?

Three to four days, according to the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service. Refrigerate it within two hours of cooking and reheat to 165°F.

Is it safe to eat food past its best before date?

Often yes, if it has been stored properly and looks, smells and tastes normal. Best before is about quality. A use-by date on raw meat or fish is about safety, so respect it.

What temperature should a fridge be?

40°F (4°C) or below. The freezer should be at 0°F (-18°C) or below. Use an appliance thermometer to check, since dials are not always accurate.

Can I freeze food right before it expires?

Yes, if it is still in good condition. Freezing stops spoilage but does not improve food, so freeze early for the best results.
