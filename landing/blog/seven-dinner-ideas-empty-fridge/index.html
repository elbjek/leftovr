Seven Dinners From an Almost Empty Fridge

An empty fridge is rarely an empty kitchen. Six staples and a few seasonings cover a full week of dinners.

By leftovr 7 min read Updated Nov 10, 2026
Short answer
You can cook seven different dinners from eggs, pasta, rice, canned beans, canned tomatoes and frozen vegetables. Each takes 10 to 25 minutes and needs only oil, salt, pepper and a few dried herbs. Below are the quantities for two people, so you can cook tonight without a shop.

Key takeaways

  • Eggs, pasta, rice, canned beans, canned tomatoes and frozen vegetables make seven distinct dinners.
  • Every recipe here serves two and takes 10 to 25 minutes.
  • Only oil, salt, pepper and dried herbs are assumed beyond the six staples.
  • Cooked rice from yesterday makes the best fried rice, so cook extra on purpose.
  • Keeping this backbone stocked is what makes an empty fridge a non-event.

An empty fridge is almost never an empty kitchen. Most homes have a bag of pasta, some rice, a few cans and something in the freezer. What is missing is not food but a plan, and the plan is what takes the stress out of 6 p.m.

This matters beyond one evening. Households are the biggest source of food waste: the UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024 puts households at 631 million tonnes of the world's wasted food in 2022, about 60 percent of the total. A dinner that uses what you already own is one less trip to the shop and one less thing forgotten at the back of a shelf.

What you need in the cupboard

Six things carry all seven dinners: eggs, dried pasta, rice, canned beans (any kind), canned chopped tomatoes and a bag of frozen vegetables. Beyond that, assume oil, salt, pepper and a few dried herbs such as oregano. An onion and a clove of garlic make everything better, but none of these recipes fail without them.

Eggs keep longer than most people think. The FDA's storage chart lists fresh eggs in the shell at 3 to 5 weeks in the fridge, so they are the safest bet to still be there when everything else is gone.

Seven dinners, with quantities

All quantities serve two. Scale up by simply doubling.

1. Tomato and bean pasta (20 minutes)

Boil 200 g of pasta. Meanwhile, warm a spoon of oil, add one 400 g can of chopped tomatoes, one drained can of beans, a teaspoon of oregano, salt and pepper. Simmer 10 minutes, then toss with the pasta and a splash of the pasta water. The beans make it filling enough to be the whole meal.

2. Egg fried rice (12 minutes)

Use about 300 g of cooked rice, ideally cold from yesterday. Fry 150 g of frozen vegetables in hot oil for 4 minutes, push them aside, scramble 3 eggs in the gap, then stir in the rice and cook until it starts to crisp. Season with salt and pepper, or soy sauce if you have it. Cold rice fries better because the grains are drier.

3. Eggs in tomato sauce (15 minutes)

Simmer one 400 g can of chopped tomatoes with oil, salt, pepper and a pinch of any dried herb for 8 minutes until it thickens. Make four wells, crack in 4 eggs, cover the pan and cook 5 to 6 minutes until the whites set. Eat with bread, rice or a spoon.

4. Frozen vegetable frittata (15 minutes)

Beat 6 eggs with salt and pepper. Cook 200 g of frozen vegetables in an oven-safe pan for 5 minutes, pour in the eggs, cook on low for 5 minutes, then finish under a hot grill for 3 to 4 minutes. If you have no grill, cover the pan and let the top set on low heat.

5. One-pan tomato rice and beans (25 minutes)

Stir 1 cup (about 200 g) of rice into a spoon of hot oil for a minute. Add one can of chopped tomatoes, one drained can of beans, 1 cup of water, salt, pepper and a teaspoon of dried herbs. Cover and simmer on low for 18 to 20 minutes until the rice is tender. Stir in a handful of frozen vegetables for the last 5 minutes.

6. Pasta with egg and peas (15 minutes)

Boil 200 g of pasta and add 100 g of frozen peas for the last 2 minutes. Beat 2 eggs with plenty of pepper. Drain, keeping a cup of the cooking water, then take the pan off the heat and toss the pasta with the eggs and a few spoons of the water until it turns glossy and coats everything. Off the heat is the trick: on the heat, the eggs scramble.

7. Tomato, bean and vegetable soup (25 minutes)

Combine one can of chopped tomatoes, one drained can of beans, 300 g of frozen vegetables, 500 ml of water, salt, pepper and herbs. Simmer 15 minutes, add a small handful of pasta or rice, and cook another 8 to 10 minutes. Mash a few beans against the side of the pot to thicken it.

Making these taste like more than pantry food

Three small habits lift every dish. First, season in layers: salt the water, salt the sauce, then taste at the end. Second, add acid at the finish, a squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar, which wakes up canned tomatoes and beans. Third, use the starchy cooking water from pasta, which turns thin sauce into something that clings.

If you have one fresh thing left, such as half an onion, a wilting pepper or a handful of herbs, add it. These recipes are templates, and a fresh scrap is exactly what they are built to absorb. For more ways to use odds and ends, see how to use leftover ingredients.

Frequently asked questions

What can I make with just eggs, rice and pasta?

Egg fried rice, pasta with egg and peas, and a plain frittata all work with these staples. Add a can of tomatoes and you can also make eggs in tomato sauce or a one-pan tomato rice.

What is the fastest dinner from pantry staples?

Egg fried rice takes about 12 minutes using cold cooked rice, an egg or three and a handful of frozen vegetables. Eggs in tomato sauce and pasta with egg and peas take about 15.

Are canned beans and frozen vegetables as good as fresh?

They are fine for cooking dinner. Canned beans are already cooked and frozen vegetables are ready to use, which is why they rescue a night with no fresh food in the house.

How do I keep an empty fridge from happening?

Keep a backbone of eggs, a grain, pasta, canned beans and tomatoes, and frozen vegetables. With those in the cupboard, dinner is always possible, so you buy fewer random items.
